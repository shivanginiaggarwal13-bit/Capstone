The **Price-Inventory Exchange Rate** is [[Supply Brain]]'s headline analytical concept: for a given SKU segment, quantify *"$1 of price flexibility = $Y of safety stock"* — a direct, comparable answer to the question "should we buffer demand uncertainty with extra stock, or with a willingness to discount?"

**Why this comparison doesn't exist in most supply-chain systems.** Inventory planning and pricing/promotions are almost always run as separate functions with separate teams and separate tools. Nobody routinely asks, at the SKU level, "what's the cheapest way to absorb this SKU's demand uncertainty — more stock, or more price flexibility?" — because doing so requires **[[Price Elasticity]]** and **[[Safety Stock]]** costing to sit inside the same model. The charter calls this out explicitly as strategic/IP value for the sponsor: "a comparison no existing supply chain system makes systematically at SKU level."

**How the exchange rate is computed.** Compare the cost of buffering demand uncertainty with inventory (holding cost + capital cost of the extra safety stock) against the cost of buffering it with price (expected discount cost required to shift enough demand to avoid the same stockout risk). A SKU with high elasticity and low holding-cost sensitivity should route toward price buffer; a SKU with low elasticity and high holding-cost sensitivity should route toward inventory buffer — most SKUs land somewhere on a spectrum between the two, not at either extreme.

**From a one-shot estimate to a self-recalibrating one.** As originally scoped, this is a single regression-based estimate computed once. The **[[Multi-Armed Bandits — Supply Brain Strategy|bandit track]]** proposes strengthening it into something the system continuously re-converges to: arms are discrete buffer-mix templates (`100% inventory / 75-25 / 50-50 / 25-75 / 100% price`) per segment, and the reward is the twin-simulated total cost of each mix. The exchange rate stops being a static number computed once at project kickoff and becomes a live, self-updating answer — which is the stronger version of the same committed deliverable, not a new one.
