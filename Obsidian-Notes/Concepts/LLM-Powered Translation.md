**LLM-Powered Translation**, in [[Supply Brain]], is a two-directional language layer: an LLM translates what a planner says in plain English into structured model parameters the system can act on, and translates structured model output back into plain-English explanations the planner can actually use.

**Why both directions matter equally.** It's tempting to think of "natural language interface" as just the input side — typing a question instead of clicking through a dashboard. But the harder and more valuable direction is often the output side: turning a Thompson Sampling belief state, a set of arm means and confidence intervals, and a twin-simulated cost comparison into a sentence a supply-chain planner (who is not a data scientist) can understand and act on immediately, without needing to know what a "belief distribution" is.

**Concrete example of the output direction, from the [[Agentic Interface]]'s `explain_recommendation` tool:** the underlying numbers — mean reward, confidence, which arms were tried how often — get turned into "For Seasonal-Beverages in peak phase, the system favors 100% inventory buffer over price flexibility, 79% confidence based on 14 simulated weeks... it favored a 50/50 hybrid in the off-season instead." No number in that sentence was invented by the LLM — it's a faithful restatement of structured data the **[[Multi-Armed Bandit|bandit engine]]** already computed.

**Why this is a translation layer, not a decision-making layer.** The LLM doesn't decide the recommendation, run the simulation, or choose the arm — all of that logic lives in the bandit engine and the **[[Digital Twin]]**. Its job is strictly interpretive in both directions: map language to structured intent, and map structured results back to language. This division of labor is what keeps the system's actual decisions auditable and testable independent of the LLM, and it's why the agentic layer's build sequencing explicitly requires the bandit engine's functions to already exist before this layer has anything real to translate.
