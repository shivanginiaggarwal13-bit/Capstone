**Non-Stationarity** is the failure mode where a [[Multi-Armed Bandit]]'s core assumption — that each arm has one fixed, unknown payout rate — stops being true, because the real payout is actually drifting over time. It is the single biggest technical risk flagged against the entire **[[Multi-Armed Bandits — Supply Brain Strategy|bandit track]]** for [[Supply Brain]].

**Why it breaks a vanilla bandit specifically, not just "adds noise."** A vanilla bandit gets *more* confident about an arm the more it's tried — that's the whole point of the exploration/exploitation mechanism. But if the true best answer changes over time and the bandit has already narrowed its belief tight from an earlier period, it becomes actively resistant to noticing the change: it's too confident to bother re-checking. **[[Exploration-Exploitation Trade-off|More confidence, in a drifting environment, is the wrong direction to move.]]**

**The concrete case in this project: seasonality.** Beverages peak in summer — stockouts are expensive there, so the inventory-buffer arm wins. The same SKU in winter has low, price-sensitive demand — the price-buffer arm wins instead. A bandit belief that's converged tight on "inventory buffer wins" from a summer run will keep recommending it into the fall.

**The fix used here: context, not drift detection.** Rather than making the bandit *discover* seasonality on its own (via a sliding window or discounted-reward mechanism reacting after the fact), the project's synthetic data generator already encodes known seasonal curves per category — so seasonal phase (peak / shoulder / trough) is fed directly as a **[[Contextual Bandit|context]]** dimension, keeping a separate belief per (segment × seasonal phase) instead of one belief per segment. A secondary safety net — a floor on how tight uncertainty is allowed to get, or a sliding window over recent weeks — still covers *unknown* shocks not on the seasonal calendar, like a surprise competitor promotion.

**Scope of the fix.** This matters most for the **Seasonal segment** (~30% of SKUs, but the largest demand amplitude in the portfolio per **[[SKU Segmentation]]**). Fast-Moving and Intermediate segments likely need only a single belief per segment — the added context dimension isn't worth the sparsity cost there.
