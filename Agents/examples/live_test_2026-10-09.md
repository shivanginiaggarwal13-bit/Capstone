**Request.**
> We expect a heatwave in the Southeast in July 2026 that should lift beverage demand by about 25% for the month, and the business will plan for it. PLANT01's liquid-fill line has planned maintenance starting 13 July and will run at 70% capacity for two weeks. Protect our fast-moving SKUs at 98.5% service during July. Also, a flood is expected to write off 30% of the opening stock at RDC03. What happens to service and working capital, and how much does re-planning help?

## What the test exposed, and what changed

1. **The gateway content filter.** Your gateway's Model Armor filter blocked one Haiku call (HTTP 400) whose prompt used role-play wording ("you are acting as the language model…"). Plain task wording passed every time. The operator prompt in `Agents/README.md` uses that wording.
2. **The validation layer works on a weak model.** Haiku sent `unit: "fraction"` for a CSL target. The tool rejected it with the exact fix and saved nothing, and Haiku corrected it on the next call. Haiku's narration also misstated z (2.09 instead of 2.17), but its final report quoted the tool's preview numbers.
3. **Carrying details between changes.** The inventory agent and the validator both turned "write off 30% of the opening stock" into a July flood, borrowing July from the heatwave. *Fix:* the shared context now says each change keeps to what the planner said about that change, and that "opening stock" means 2026-W01.
4. **The validator skipped its evidence.** It gave a verdict from the brief alone and missed that the capacity cut never binds. A stricter prompt did not change this on Haiku. *Fix (in code):* the validator's graph now starts with an `evidence` node that runs `list_overrides` and `validate_draft` before the first model call. On re-test, the Haiku validator flagged the non-binding cut with the right numbers (peak load 5,280 units under the reduced capacity).
5. **A remaining reasoning slip.** Even with the evidence, the Haiku validator claimed that +25% becomes "1.25 × 1.07 = 1.34× baseline". That is wrong, because the baseline realises the same 1.07×. The Sonnet orchestrator caught and corrected it.

**Recommendation.** Keep Haiku for the authoring specialists: they were accurate, and the validation layer catches their slips. Run the **validator on Sonnet**. That is one line in `runtime/profiles.yaml` (`validator_agent: {model: claude-sonnet-4-6}`), left for the team to decide.

The full request and response files for every call, readable as markdown, are in `Agents/workspace/bridge/` (git-ignored) for anyone re-checking this run.
