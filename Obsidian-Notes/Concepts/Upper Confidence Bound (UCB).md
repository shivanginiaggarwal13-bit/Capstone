**Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)** is a [[Multi-Armed Bandit]] algorithm that plays the arm with the highest value of "mean reward so far + a confidence bonus," where the bonus shrinks the more times that arm has already been tried. The principle behind it is often summarized as **"optimism in the face of uncertainty"** — an arm you know little about gets a generous bonus that makes it look more attractive than its raw average alone would suggest, which is what pulls exploration toward under-tried arms without needing a random coin flip the way **[[Epsilon-Greedy]]** does.

**How it differs from [[Thompson Sampling]].** Both react to uncertainty rather than ignoring it, but UCB is **deterministic** — given the same history, it always picks the same arm — while Thompson Sampling is **stochastic**, drawing a random sample from each belief every round. UCB's deterministic score is easier to reason about theoretically (there are cleaner mathematical regret guarantees), but it's harder to translate into the kind of plain-language "confidence" statement the **[[Agentic Interface]]**'s `explain_recommendation` tool needs — "the upper confidence bound was higher" is a less intuitive explanation for a planner than Thompson Sampling's "79% confidence based on 14 simulated weeks."

**Why it wasn't chosen as the default for [[Supply Brain]].** Not ruled out on technical grounds — UCB is a legitimate, well-studied alternative — but Thompson Sampling was preferred specifically for its natural fit to two project-specific requirements: explainability to a non-technical planner, and clean extension to **[[Contextual Bandit|contextual]]** beliefs keyed on segment × seasonal phase for handling **[[Non-Stationarity (Bandits)|seasonality]]**.

**Where it remains relevant.** As a documented alternative in the algorithm comparison (see **[[Multi-Armed Bandits — Supply Brain Strategy]] §3**) — worth revisiting if Thompson Sampling's stochastic behavior proves harder to validate/debug during Phase 3 engine development than UCB's deterministic one would be.
