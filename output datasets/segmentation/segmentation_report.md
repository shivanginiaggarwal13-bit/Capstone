# Supply Brain — SKU Segmentation

## Executive summary

This is the canonical segmentation output for the current regenerated dataset. The v5 service-level-focused pipeline evaluates SKU value, demand variability, forecastability, and supply risk, then assigns each SKU to a business archetype and recommended service-level policy.

The run covers **500 SKUs** and selects **six clusters** using bootstrap stability. The two core groups contain 267 SKUs (53.4% of the catalogue) but carry 92.2% of revenue and 92.7% of net margin. This concentration supports differentiated inventory protection: core items deserve the strongest availability focus, while low-value tail items should receive leaner defaults and targeted exceptions.

**Read the archetype names with care.** They are generated by rule (`label_archetypes()` in `segmentation_v5.py`), and several do not match the cluster's actual signature in `sku_clusters.csv` and `cluster_service_levels.csv`:

- **Long-Tail Supply-Risk (cluster 5)** has the **highest baseline WMAPE** in the portfolio (1.85). It is a forecast-challenged group. Its mean lead-time CV (0.4805) differs from Long-Tail Erratic's (0.4804) by less than 0.0001. The name comes from a strict "highest LT CV" rule with no magnitude test, and its `if/elif` structure stops the cluster from also being called Forecast-Challenged. No cluster in this run carries the Forecast-Challenged name.
- **Long-Tail Erratic 2 (cluster 2)** has the **lowest lead-time CV** of all six clusters (0.437 vs about 0.48 elsewhere). Its demand CV (0.52) is below every other tail cluster's, so it is not erratic. The SHAP surrogate also picks `lead_time_cv` as its dominant driver.
- **Long-Tail Erratic 3 (cluster 3)** is defined by promo-week forecast failure: mean promo-lift WMAPE is 50.9 vs 0.33–2.52 elsewhere, typical of sparse promo-week actuals. It is not defined by demand variability.
- **Long-Tail Erratic (cluster 4)** does have the highest base-demand CV (0.80).
- **Long-Tail Erratic 2's 95.7% CSL** comes from a threshold edge. Its revenue share is 0.030044, just above the 3% cap threshold (`revenue_share <= 0.03`). It misses the 90% cap and keeps its median newsvendor ratio of 0.957. Long-Tail Erratic, with 2.4% of revenue, gets 90%.
- **Flagship Core and Mid-Tier Core both get 97.0%**, because both clear the 30% revenue-share floor. The tier label carries no policy difference.

## Business use

- Protect Flagship Core and Mid-Tier Core first; they carry almost all portfolio economics.
- Treat Mid-Tier Core promotions separately: its promotional volume share is the highest in the portfolio (36%).
- Investigate forecast process, sparse demand and promo response for the forecast-challenged tail groups before adding blanket inventory. These are Long-Tail Supply-Risk (baseline WMAPE 1.85) and Long-Tail Erratic 3 (promo-lift WMAPE 50.9).
- For Long-Tail Supply-Risk, start with the forecast, not sourcing (see above). Its lead-time variability is no different from the rest of the portfolio.
- Use the 90% CSL groups as candidates for assortment, order-frequency, or make-to-order decisions, not automatic deletion.

## Method and controls

The model uses eight active features in four equally weighted blocks: VALUE, VARIABILITY, FORECASTABILITY, and SUPPLY_RISK.

- **Perishability is excluded by design.** The script excludes perishability features (`shelf_life_days`, `write_off_rate`) because shelf life speaks to obsolescence and overstock cost, not to the fill-rate decision a service-level target encodes (`EXCLUDED_BY_DESIGN` in `segmentation_v5.py`). For consistency, the obsolescence term is also removed from the newsvendor overstock cost. Separately, this dataset has no live expiry trade-off anyway: shelf life is 1,095–1,458 days against a 1,092-day history, and `write_offs.csv` is empty.
- **`otd_pct` is pruned** because it is near-constant (CV 0.0159, below the 0.02 cutoff).
- **The two surviving SUPPLY_RISK features are weak.** `lead_time_mean_days` (CV 0.032) and `lead_time_cv` (CV 0.037) only just clear the cutoff, yet together carry 25% of the distance metric. Both are derived from `inventory.cum_lead_time_mean/std_days`, which is constant across all 156 weeks for each SKU × DC. They therefore encode which DCs a SKU flows through (a static network parameter), not realised supply variability.
- **k selection.** The selected k=6 solution has a stability ARI of 0.848, the highest in the 4–8 range (procedure in [Feature engineering](#feature-engineering), step 6). Silhouette is low (0.171; k=5 was slightly higher at 0.176), so boundary SKUs should receive business review.

The newsvendor critical ratio uses:
- understock cost = list-price unit margin × the subcategory stockout multiplier;
- overstock cost = 0.5 × annual holding cost.

That ratio is then combined with value-based CSL floors and caps based on cluster **revenue** share:
- revenue share ≥ 30% → floor of 97%;
- revenue share ≤ 3% → cap of 90%;
- otherwise, the cluster's median ratio, clipped to [0.85, 0.995].

Because the synthetic cost inputs produce economically similar critical ratios (cluster medians 0.950–0.957), the practical service ladder is primarily strategic. Recalibrate it when real expiry, penalty, substitution, or customer-priority costs are available.

## Feature engineering

All features are built at **SKU grain**, one row per SKU in `sku_master` (500 in this run), by `build_feature_table()` in `segmentation_v5.py`. Each feature function aggregates its source table to `sku_id`, and the result is left-joined onto `sku_master`. A SKU missing from a source therefore gets a blank value, which median imputation fills later. The script engineers 11 features: the 9 candidates in four blocks (`BLOCKS_CANDIDATE`) and the 2 perishability features. The perishability features are computed and written to `sku_clusters.csv`, but they never enter pruning or clustering (`EXCLUDED_BY_DESIGN`; see Method and controls).

**Source windows.** The source tables do not cover the same weeks:
- `orders`: every period present, 156 weeks. "Annual" values are multiplied by 52 ÷ (number of distinct `orders.period` values) = 52/156 = 1/3. This is `annualization_factor()`; `feature_value()` computes the same factor inline.
- `demand_forecast`: only rows with `horizon == "in_sample"`, which are the 52 weeks of 2025. The `forward` horizon is not used.
- `inventory`: all rows, but `cum_lead_time_*` is constant across weeks for each SKU × DC, so the window makes no difference.
- `replenishment_receipts`: all receipts, with no period filter.

The feature functions have fallbacks for older dataset layouts: revenue minus standard cost, a promo flag rolled up from `orders.on_promo`, capacity-weighted `network_lanes` lead times, and `otd_pct` marked UNAVAILABLE. The current dataset uses the primary path in every case. Recomputing the features that way from the input data reproduces `sku_clusters.csv` to floating-point precision.

### Candidate features

"CV" is the statistic the pruning test uses: population standard deviation ÷ mean across the 500 SKUs, on raw (unwinsorised) values. It is recomputed here from `sku_clusters.csv`; `dropped_features.csv` records it only for `otd_pct`.

| Feature | Block | Source columns | Window | SKU-level formula | Units | CV | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---:|---|
| `log_annual_net_margin` | VALUE | `orders.net_margin_usd` | 156 wk, × 52/156 | ln(max(annual net margin, floor)); floor = max(p1 of annual net margin across SKUs, $1) | ln(USD / year) | 0.186 | Kept |
| `net_margin_pct` | VALUE | `orders.net_margin_usd`, `net_revenue_usd` | 156 wk | Σ net margin ÷ Σ net revenue (blank if revenue is 0) | ratio | 0.283 | Kept |
| `demand_cv_base` | VARIABILITY | `orders.order_qty`, `on_promo`, `period` | 156 wk | Non-promo lines only: weekly SKU quantity summed across DCs and customers, then σ ÷ μ over weeks (0 if μ = 0) | ratio | 0.478 | Kept |
| `promo_depth_share` | VARIABILITY | `orders.order_qty`, `on_promo` | 156 wk | Σ promo quantity ÷ Σ quantity (0 if no promo lines) | share of volume | 0.321 | Kept |
| `forecast_baseline_wmape` | FORECASTABILITY | `demand_forecast.actual_qty`, `forecast_qty`, `forecast_promo_lift_qty`, `horizon` | in-sample, 52 wk | Σ\|actual − forecast\| ÷ Σ actual over non-promo rows (blank if Σ actual = 0) | ratio | 0.840 | Kept |
| `forecast_lift_wmape` | FORECASTABILITY | as above | in-sample, 52 wk | Same, over promo-week rows; blank → the SKU's baseline WMAPE | ratio | 4.479 | Kept |
| `lead_time_mean_days` | SUPPLY_RISK | `inventory.cum_lead_time_mean_days`; `orders.order_qty` as weights | static; weights 156 wk | Mean per SKU × DC, then order-quantity-weighted mean across the SKU's DCs | days | 0.032 | Kept |
| `lead_time_cv` | SUPPLY_RISK | `inventory.cum_lead_time_mean_days`, `cum_lead_time_std_days`; `orders.order_qty` | static; weights 156 wk | std ÷ mean per SKU × DC, then order-quantity-weighted mean across DCs | ratio | 0.037 | Kept |
| `otd_pct` | SUPPLY_RISK | `replenishment_receipts.on_time_flag`, `qty_ordered` | all receipts | Σ(on_time_flag × qty_ordered) ÷ Σ qty_ordered | share | 0.0159 | Dropped: near-constant |
| `shelf_life_days` | PERISHABILITY | `sku_master.shelf_life_days` | static | As recorded | days | (0.084) | Excluded by design |
| `write_off_rate` | PERISHABILITY | `write_offs.units_written_off`; `inventory.shipments` | all rows | Σ written off ÷ Σ shipments (missing → 0) | share | zero variance | Excluded by design |

The two perishability features never reach the pruning test; their statistics are shown only for reference.

**VALUE.** Margin is the line-level `net_margin_usd`, which the script's docstring describes as net of off-invoice, trade fund, volume rebate and logistics allowance. Promo weeks are included. The log floor is $221.02 in this run: values below the 1st percentile are raised to it before the log, which affects 5 SKUs. `net_margin_pct` divides two annualised sums, so the annualisation cancels. The newsvendor ratio uses a different margin: `sku_master.margin_usd`, the list-price unit margin.

**VARIABILITY.** `demand_cv_base` keeps only order lines with `on_promo == False`. It sums them per SKU and week across all DCs and customers, then takes σ (population, ddof 0) ÷ μ over the weeks that have at least one non-promo line. Weeks with no non-promo orders are left out, not counted as zero, so intermittency is not measured as variability. A week in which some customers were on promo still contributes its non-promo lines, so the base volume for that week is partial. Pooling across DCs and customers makes this a network-level CV, not a DC-level one. `promo_depth_share` is the share of 156-week order volume sold on promo. Despite its name, it does not use discount depth (`discount_pct`).

**FORECASTABILITY.** Rows are at SKU × DC × customer × week grain. A row counts as a promo week when `forecast_promo_lift_qty` > 0, with blanks treated as 0. That is the forecast's own promo flag, not `orders.on_promo`. Absolute errors and actuals are summed over all of the SKU's rows in each subset. A row with zero actual adds its forecast to the numerator and nothing to the denominator, so sparse customer-level series push WMAPE up. In `sku_clusters.csv` the maxima are 4.35 for baseline WMAPE and 169.3 for promo-week WMAPE. If a SKU has no promo-week rows, or its promo-week actuals sum to zero, `forecast_lift_wmape` is set equal to its baseline WMAPE. That applies to 80 SKUs, the rows in `sku_clusters.csv` where the two values are identical.

**SUPPLY_RISK.** For each SKU × DC, the script averages `cum_lead_time_mean_days` and `cum_lead_time_std_days` over the inventory rows, and the DC CV is std ÷ mean. The SKU value is the average over its DCs, weighted by 156-week `orders.order_qty` at each DC. DCs with no volume or no lead-time data get no weight. `lead_time_cv` is therefore a volume-weighted average of per-DC CVs, not the CV of observed lead times. `otd_pct` pools every receipt of the SKU across all DCs and origins.

**PERISHABILITY (excluded).** `shelf_life_days` is copied from `sku_master`. `write_off_rate` is 0 for every SKU because `write_offs.csv` is empty.

### From features to clusters

1. **Pruning** (`prune_features()`, on raw values). Design exclusions are logged first. Each candidate is then tested in order. If every value is blank, it is dropped as UNAVAILABLE. If its population variance is ≤ 1e-6, it is dropped as STATISTICAL (zero variance). If |σ ÷ μ| < 0.02, it is dropped as STATISTICAL (near-constant). A block with no survivors would be removed entirely. In this run 8 of the 9 candidates survive, 2 per block (`dropped_features.csv`, `run_metadata.json`).
2. **Median imputation.** Blank values in surviving features are filled with that feature's median across all SKUs. No surviving feature has blanks in this run, so this step changes nothing. The promo-week WMAPE fallback above happens earlier, inside feature engineering.
3. **Winsorisation** at p1/p99 for each feature (`winsor_pct=0.01`). In this run it clips 5 SKUs at each end of every feature. For example, promo-week WMAPE is capped at 47.0 and baseline WMAPE at 2.18. The module docstring says scaling happens before winsorisation, but the code winsorises first.
4. **Standardisation** with `StandardScaler`: mean 0 and population SD 1, fitted on the winsorised 500 × 8 matrix.
5. **Block weighting.** Each standardised value z becomes z · √(1 / (n_blocks · n_in_block)). With 4 blocks of 2 features, every weight is √(1/8) ≈ 0.354, and each block carries 25.0% of total variance (`cluster_feature_block_balance.csv`). Every block has two features in this run, so the weighting is a uniform rescale that leaves the cluster geometry unchanged. It would matter if pruning left blocks of unequal size. For example, had `otd_pct` survived, each SUPPLY_RISK feature would get √(1/12).
6. **k selection** (`select_k()`). For each k from 4 to 8, the script fits `KMeans(n_clusters=k, random_state=42, n_init=10)` on all SKUs and records the silhouette. For stability, it draws 25 subsamples of 80% of SKUs (400) without replacement. The subsample generator is re-seeded with 42 for every k, so every k sees the same subsamples. Each subsample is re-clustered with `KMeans(random_state=42+b, n_init=5)`, where b is the subsample index, and compared by Adjusted Rand Index (ARI) with the full-fit labels of the same SKUs. The k with the highest mean ARI wins; silhouette only breaks ties.

7. **Final clusters.** The final labels are the full-sample k=6 fit from step 6: k-means++ initialisation and Euclidean distance on the block-weighted matrix. A one-way ANOVA per surviving feature, on raw values, is written to `cluster_anova.csv`.

**After clustering.** `service_ladder()` and `assign_csl()` are summarised under Method and controls. A few further details:
- `cost_parameters` is at DC × subcategory grain and has no category column. The script averages it across DCs within each subcategory before joining, so the stockout multiplier is a mean across DCs. The storage and carrying-cost columns are averaged too, but they are not used.
- Cluster revenue share uses annualised `net_revenue_usd`. The annualisation cancels in the share.
- The CSL is rounded to 3 decimals. Target case fill = CSL − 0.02, and safety factor z = Φ⁻¹(CSL).

**Caveats.**
- **Lead time is static.** Both SUPPLY_RISK features come from `cum_lead_time_*`, which never changes across weeks. They measure a SKU's DC mix, not realised supply variability (see Method and controls).
- **WMAPE is measured at customer grain and includes zero-actual rows.** This inflates WMAPE for sparse SKUs. Promo-week WMAPE is also computed over a subset of each SKU's rows, so a few large errors against a small actual total can dominate it. That is why winsorisation matters for the FORECASTABILITY block.
- **`promo_depth_share` is promo volume share**, not discount depth.
- **Windows differ.** VALUE, VARIABILITY and the lead-time weights use 156 weeks of orders. FORECASTABILITY uses only the 52 in-sample weeks, so a SKU's forecast error and its margin cover different periods.
- **`demand_cv_base` ignores weeks with no non-promo orders**, so it understates intermittent demand.
